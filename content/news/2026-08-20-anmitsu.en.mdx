Since August began, every conversation in the kitchen has been about the well.
The water is not getting as cold as it should this year, and on some days the
mizu-yōkan has to sit an hour longer before it can be cut.

What came out of that is the buckwheat groat anmitsu. In place of the usual
rice-flour dumplings, we scatter boiled groats through the agar.

## Why the dumplings went

Rice-flour dumplings melt. Melting dumplings, melting agar, smooth bean paste —
all three were pulling in the same direction, and there was nothing in the bowl
to bite.

Buckwheat groats keep a core even after boiling. They make a small sound when
you chew. That one thing changed the whole dish.

## How long to boil them

Eight minutes. At ten they collapse; at six they taste of raw flour. It shifts
with how dry that batch of grain is, so a few get boiled and chewed every
morning before service.

On the list until the middle of September.
